About Me

Applied Mathematician, Researcher, and Educator

Biography

I am Jordy Cevallos, an applied mathematician whose research focuses on mathematical modeling in biological, epidemiological, and social systems. I am currently pursuing a Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics for the Life and Social Sciences at Arizona State University, where I previously earned an M.S. in the same program.

My work combines differential equations, dynamical systems, optimization, and data-driven approaches to investigate complex real-world problems. Over the years, I have worked on projects involving infectious disease transmission, vector-borne diseases, social dynamics, ecological systems, and population biology.

Originally from Ecuador, I earned my Bachelor's degree in Mathematics from Yachay Tech University, graduating Cum Laude. My academic journey has taken me from nonlinear partial differential equations to interdisciplinary applications of mathematics in public health and the life sciences.

Current Research

My current research investigates the transmission dynamics of infectious diseases and the role of human behavior in shaping epidemic outcomes. Recent projects include mathematical models for dengue prevention strategies, HPV transmission and cervical cancer risk, COVID-19 dynamics, and social hierarchy formation in biological systems.

I am particularly interested in integrating mathematical theory with real-world data to develop models that can inform public health interventions and policy decisions.

Teaching

I am passionate about teaching mathematics and helping students develop strong quantitative reasoning skills. Throughout my graduate studies, I have taught and assisted with courses ranging from introductory mathematical modeling to advanced applied mathematics at Arizona State University and Chandler-Gilbert Community College.

My teaching philosophy emphasizes conceptual understanding, problem-solving, and connecting mathematical ideas to real-world applications.
